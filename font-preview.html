Six typefaces, your actual copy

Every block below renders the real hero, a case-study heading, body copy and a metric — so you're judging the font on your words, not on "The quick brown fox". Scroll, pick the one that reads best, and tell me the name. The site is currently set to Inter.

01 · Inter — currently applied

The default of serious software — Linear, Vercel, GitHub, Figma. Invisible in a good way: nobody has ever described a site as badly designed because it used Inter. Designed specifically for screens, so it stays sharp at small sizes in your tables and labels. The trade-off is character: it's safe, not memorable.

02 · Plus Jakarta Sans

Inter with warmth. Slightly rounder, a touch more personality in the a, g and y, and headlines feel a little friendlier without becoming quirky. Popular in modern SaaS marketing sites. If Inter feels too neutral to you, this is the smallest safe step away from it.

04 · Manrope

Semi-rounded and open, with noticeably wide letterforms. Headlines feel calm and confident rather than punchy. Best of these for long reading — your case studies are long, and this is the easiest on the eye across a 2,000-word page. Slightly less crisp than Inter in small table text.

06 · Source Serif headings + Inter body

The one genuinely different direction: serif headlines over a sans body. Signals senior, considered, editorial — closer to a strategy consultancy than a tech startup. It's the boldest choice here, and the one most likely to make a recruiter remember the page. It slightly undersells the "I write firmware" half of your positioning.
